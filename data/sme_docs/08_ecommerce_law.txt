Cambodia's E-Commerce Law and Digital Business Rules

Cambodia adopted its Law on Electronic Commerce in 2019, establishing the first comprehensive legal framework for online business activity in the country. The law gives legal recognition to electronic records, electronic signatures, and electronic contracts, meaning that agreements concluded online are enforceable in the same way as paper contracts. It also sets obligations for e-commerce service providers and intermediaries, including requirements to operate honestly, protect consumer interests, and secure customer data.

Businesses that sell goods or services online, including through websites and social media shops, are expected to obtain appropriate e-commerce permits or licenses from the Ministry of Commerce, with simplified requirements for small sellers. The law works alongside consumer protection rules that prohibit misleading advertising and unfair practices. For Cambodian SMEs, the framework matters because a large share of online selling happens through Facebook, Telegram, and other social channels; the law brings this activity into the formal economy and gives buyers and sellers legal certainty in disputes.
